# Sony-A-mount-Disposable-Camera-Lens
This repo contains STL files and instructions for creating a simple '[dispo len](https://focus.picfair.com/articles/i-used-a-50-lens-to-turn-my-digital-camera-into-a-disposable-camera-and-was-amazed-by-the-results)' or a tri-lens '[wigglegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiggle_stereoscopy)' for Sony A-mount. The final assembly consists of six printed parts which can be assembled without screws. ABS filament is preferred. Solidpart files are included for those who wish to modify the design.

# Overview

The goal of this project was to create a lens to emulate the look of the [Nishika N8000](https://www.lomography.com/magazine/67150-nishika-n8000-whos-ugly-cousin-is-this), a niche film camera from 1989. It used four lenses to partition the image into vertical strips, which could then be layered on top of one another to create a psudo-3D image. The idea and general method emulating this came from a [<ins>blog post</ins>](https://www.diyphotography.net/how-i-made-a-3d-printed-wigglegram-lens-from-three-disposable-cameras/) by Joshua Bird (which has examples of these types of images). While his method was for a Canon Film Camera, the general idea of using small plastic lenses salvaged from disposable cameras was the same for my Sony α58, which has the cost advantage of shooting digital images instead of physical film. The disadvantage of my Sony was the space constraints that would be challenging while designing this lens. Ultimately, I was able to create a triple lens camera by sanding down generic wedding disposble camera lenses (which are smaller than Fujifilm branded ones, for example) to conserve space. While the main focus of this project was using the triple lenses to create the psudo-3D images, I also designed a version for a single disposable camera lens, since that was trivially extra work.

# Instructions 
* Obtain 1-3 recycled disposable cameras and extract the lens(es)
  * **Note**: When I bought recycled cameras in bulk, I noticed that Fujifilm ones have slightly larger lenses. I designed the single lens insert for a Fujifilm or equivalent lens, and the triple enclosure for smaller generic lenses (like the ones sold for weddings) that need to be sanded down until no plastic surrounds the convex lens on the sides. 
* Print the primary parts and desired inserts 
  * **Note**: black ABS filament at 0.4mm for the larger parts and 0.2m for the smaller parts is recommended
* Assemble the pieces:
  * **Note**: The final lens does not twist on and off like a typical lens due to how far into the body it inserts. The exact process is described below. The part names referred to below can be found in the downloadable file list
* Place the clear plastic lens(es) into the Lens Holder Rear (Triple/Single)
* Hold the Lens Holder Front (Triple/Single) in front of the lens(es) 
* Hold the Light Shield Triple/Lens Holder Spacer behind the Lens Holder Rear
* Slide these parts into the Insert
  * **Note**:You could probably glue this together, but it held well with just friction for me, allowing me to prototype and swap between the triple and single lens relatively easily
* Twist the Bayonet onto the camera body, align the hole with the red dot
* **Raise the mirror in your camera so it lays flat up in provided space the and enable shooting with the mirror raised in your camera settings**
* Slide the Insert into the Bayonet with the line on the back of the insert down
* Twist the Outer Ring onto the Bayonet to lock everything together

# Final Thoughts
<img width="730" height="233" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ce5af56d-2ebc-48be-b63c-0db6cb0f135c" />

While the mounting and attachment of the parts are unconventional, it was satisfying to undertake the creation of  a lens that was theoretically impossible due to the large A-mount flange distance and the small width of my APS-C sensor (67% of the smallest width I've ever seen this done on). To create the '3D' gifs shown above, I recommend following a tutorial like [this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UhiG_lI-StE). I personally used Gimp for my editing with jpeg photos, and added retro film effects using this [free plugin](https://gmic.eu/download.html). You can easily convert these gifs to mp4 files for use in any editing program or to post online.
